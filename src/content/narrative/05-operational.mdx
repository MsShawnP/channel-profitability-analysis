import ChannelChart from '../../components/charts/ChannelChart';
import layers from '../../data/layers.json';
import channels from '../../data/channels.json';
export const netLayer = layers[4];
export const retailers = netLayer.channels.filter(c => c.channel_type === 'retailer');
export const distributors = netLayer.channels.filter(c => c.channel_type === 'distributor');
export const dtc = netLayer.channels.filter(c => c.channel_type === 'DTC');
export const totalDisputes = channels.reduce((sum, c) => sum + c.disputes_filed, 0);

## {netLayer.label}

<p class="section-subtitle">{netLayer.subtitle}</p>

Every deduction and fine generates downstream work: someone has to triage the claim, decide whether to dispute, file the paperwork, and track the resolution. This labour is real cost that rarely appears in channel profitability models.

Cinderhaven files {totalDisputes.toLocaleString()} disputes per year across all channels (annual average, FY2024–FY2026) — roughly 1,800 from retailers and 250 from distributors. At $35 per hour fully loaded, that dispute workload totals $147.0K/yr in operational overhead, invisible on the income statement but real in headcount.

Walmart accounts for $27.5K/yr of that overhead, the highest of any channel — 381 disputes per year consuming ~787 hours of finance-team time. Kroger is close behind at $26.3K/yr on ~366 disputes. Among distributors, dispute volumes are far lower, reflecting simpler deduction profiles (five deduction types versus nine for retailers).
