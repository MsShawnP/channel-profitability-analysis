import ChannelChart from '../../components/charts/ChannelChart';

export const netLayer = layers[4];
export const retailers = netLayer.channels.filter(c => c.channel_type === 'retailer');
export const distributors = netLayer.channels.filter(c => c.channel_type === 'distributor');
export const dtc = netLayer.channels.filter(c => c.channel_type === 'DTC');
export const totalDisputes = channels.reduce((sum, c) => sum + c.disputes_filed, 0);

## {netLayer.label}

<p class="section-subtitle">{netLayer.subtitle}</p>

Every deduction and fine generates downstream work: someone has to triage the claim, decide whether to dispute, file the paperwork, and track the resolution. This labour is real cost that rarely appears in channel profitability models.

Cinderhaven filed {totalDisputes.toLocaleString()} disputes across all channels in the year to March 2026. Between triage, investigation, filing, and resolution, each dispute averages over four hours of finance-team time at $35 per hour fully loaded — $137K in operational overhead, invisible on the income statement but real in headcount.

That $137K generated only $11K in recoveries — a recovery-to-cost ratio of 0.08:1. The dispute process costs roughly twelve times what it recovers. Returns are poor across all channels: Costco, the strongest performer, recovers 10 cents per dollar spent on triage. No channel breaks even.

Costco accounts for $24K of that overhead, driven by volume: 159 disputes on 357 deduction events. Walmart is close behind at $22K on 144 disputes. Among distributors, UNFI and KeHE together generated $10K in triage costs — modest totals reflecting fewer deduction types and lower dispute volumes.
