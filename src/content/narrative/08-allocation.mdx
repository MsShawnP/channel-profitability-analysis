import layers from '../../data/layers.json';

export const netLayer = layers[4];
export const totalContribution = netLayer.channels.reduce((s, c) => s + c.value, 0);
export const walmartContribution = netLayer.channels.find(c => c.channel_name === 'Walmart')?.value || 0;
export const walmartShare = ((walmartContribution / totalContribution) * 100).toFixed(0);
export const dtcContribution = netLayer.channels.find(c => c.channel_name === 'DTC')?.value || 0;
export const dtcShare = ((dtcContribution / totalContribution) * 100).toFixed(0);

## What this means for capital allocation

The numbers above point to three actions, in order of expected return.

**1. Restructure dispute triage.** Operational overhead from dispute resolution totals $137K — yet recoveries are just $11K, a 0.08:1 return. The process costs roughly twelve times what it recovers. The largest single cost category in the waterfall after COGS, dispute overhead is the clearest candidate for reduction: automate low-value claims, raise the filing threshold, or eliminate disputes on channels where recovery is negligible.

**2. Grow DTC and distributor volume.** DTC (92% margin) and distributors (89–90%) both retain far more per dollar than retailers (72–79%). DTC carries the additional advantage of zero deductions, zero fines, and zero dispute overhead. At $367K in revenue, DTC is still small enough that modest growth delivers outsized contribution gains.

**3. Review Costco trade terms.** Costco has the lowest contribution margin (72.1%), the highest trade deduction rate (2.1% of revenue), and the highest operational overhead ($24K). It is the weakest channel on every cost metric. A trade-term renegotiation or promotional rebalancing could close part of the seven-point gap between Costco and the best-performing retailers.
