import layers from '../../data/layers.json';

export const netLayer = layers[4];
export const totalContribution = netLayer.channels.reduce((s, c) => s + c.value, 0);
export const walmartContribution = netLayer.channels.find(c => c.channel_name === 'Walmart')?.value || 0;
export const walmartShare = ((walmartContribution / totalContribution) * 100).toFixed(0);
export const dtcContribution = netLayer.channels.find(c => c.channel_name === 'DTC')?.value || 0;
export const dtcShare = ((dtcContribution / totalContribution) * 100).toFixed(0);

## What this means for capital allocation

The numbers above point to three actions, in order of expected return.

**1. Grow distributor volume.** Distributors retain 90 cents of every dollar — the highest margins in the portfolio, nine to ten points above retailers. COGS runs at half the retailer rate, deduction profiles are simpler (five types versus nine), and dispute volumes are a fraction of retailer levels. At $8.2M/yr, the distributor segment already contributes $7.4M/yr. Incremental volume here delivers the highest marginal return of any channel type.

**2. Restructure retailer dispute triage.** Operational overhead from dispute resolution totals $147.0K/yr, driven almost entirely by retailers: Walmart alone accounts for $27.5K/yr on ~381 disputes per year. Automate low-value claims, raise the filing threshold, or eliminate disputes on channels where recovery is negligible. This is the largest controllable cost in the retailer waterfall after COGS.

**3. Review Costco economics.** Costco has the lowest contribution margin (79.6%) and the highest trade deduction rate (1.1% of revenue). It is the weakest retailer on margin. A trade-term renegotiation or promotional rebalancing could close part of the three-point gap between Costco and the best-performing retailers.
