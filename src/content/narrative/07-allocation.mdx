import layers from '../../data/layers.json';

export const netLayer = layers[4];
export const totalContribution = netLayer.channels.reduce((s, c) => s + c.value, 0);
export const walmartContribution = netLayer.channels.find(c => c.channel_name === 'Walmart')?.value || 0;
export const walmartShare = ((walmartContribution / totalContribution) * 100).toFixed(0);
export const dtcContribution = netLayer.channels.find(c => c.channel_name === 'DTC')?.value || 0;
export const dtcShare = ((dtcContribution / totalContribution) * 100).toFixed(0);

## What this means for capital allocation

The numbers above point to three actions, in order of expected return.

**1. Accelerate DTC growth.** DTC contributes 73 cents per revenue dollar — more than three times what wholesale channels deliver. At $3.9M in revenue, it already contributes {dtcShare}% of total net contribution on just 5.6% of revenue. The margin advantage is structural (full retail pricing, zero deductions, zero fines), not temporary. Every additional DTC dollar is worth three wholesale dollars in contribution terms.

**2. Reduce spoilage across distributors.** Spoilage fines total $1.4M — more than all other compliance categories combined. UNFI alone accounts for $446K across 545 events. This signals a cold-chain or shelf-life problem that warrants joint investigation with the distributor partners. Unlike label fines (fixable with a QA gate), spoilage requires operational changes to packaging, logistics timing, or product mix.

**3. Root-cause unclassified deductions.** "Unclassified" chargebacks total $3.0M across all channels. These are deductions Cinderhaven cannot explain from remittance data alone. Some portion is legitimate; some portion is recoverable. Closing the documentation gap (matching each deduction to a root cause) is the prerequisite to disputing or preventing them. At Walmart, $1.2M in vague deductions represents an outsized recovery opportunity.
