## {finesLayer.label}

<p class="section-subtitle">{finesLayer.subtitle}</p>

Compliance fines are the second invisible tax. Unlike trade deductions — which reflect commercial terms — fines penalise operational failures: mislabelled cases, damaged pallets, late trucks, spoiled product. They signal process gaps, not negotiating outcomes.

Walmart's $249K in fines leads in absolute terms, but Costco's fine structure is more punitive per incident. Costco levied $51K in label fines across just 16 events — roughly $3,200 per violation. Walmart's label fines average $469 each. The implication: a single Costco labelling error costs as much as seven at Walmart.

Label fines ($222K across all channels) are the largest fine category — larger than spoilage ($125K), late delivery ($114K), and damaged goods ($78K) combined minus spoilage. This is a process problem, not a logistics problem: labels are controllable at the warehouse.
