## Margin evolution

<p class="section-subtitle">Thirteen quarters, Q1 2023 through Q1 2026</p>

Contribution margins across wholesale channels range from 42% to 57%. Retailers land between 46% and 57% quarter to quarter; distributors run lower at 42–47%. Quarter-to-quarter variation is modest — rarely more than two to three points — driven by fluctuations in deduction intensity and overhead timing.

Among retailers, Whole Foods has the strongest margin (55.6%), while Costco has the weakest (46.8%). The gap between the best and worst retailer in any given quarter is roughly nine points — wider than it appears, driven primarily by differences in COGS intensity and trade deduction rates.

The mid-tier retailers (Kroger, Sprouts, Regional Group) cluster between 50% and 53%. No persistent structural gap separates them — the spread is driven by short-term deduction timing rather than permanent cost differences.

Among distributors, UNFI, KeHE, and DPI Northwest cluster at 44–47% contribution margins — several points below the retailer average. The conventional assumption that distribution is the cheaper route does not survive the full cost stack. DPI Northwest is the weakest quarter to quarter at 43.3%.

DTC margins hold near 53% year-round. The channel carries no trade deductions or compliance fines, but direct fulfilment, platform and payment fees, and customer returns absorb roughly 30 cents of every revenue dollar — costs the wholesale channels never see. Its COGS ratio runs at just 17.4%, well below wholesale, because DTC sells at full retail pricing on the same product cost base. The two effects roughly offset: DTC lands in the same low-50s range as the stronger retailers, not above them.

COGS ratio applied from annual channel averages. Compliance fines and operational overhead are excluded from the quarterly view due to reporting lags. Click any line to isolate a channel.</p>
