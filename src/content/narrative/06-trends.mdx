## Margin evolution

<p class="section-subtitle">Five quarters, Q1 2025 through Q1 2026</p>

Contribution margins across wholesale channels cluster in the 19–35% range — reflecting the structural reality that COGS consumes more than half of every wholesale dollar before deductions even begin. Quarter-to-quarter variation is driven almost entirely by deduction intensity.

Among retailers, Costco shows the most volatility: margins swing from 22% to 32% depending on the quarter's deduction load. A single quarter of concentrated promo billbacks or slotting fees can move the margin by ten points on Costco's revenue base. Walmart is more stable (19–22%), but at its scale, even a two-point move represents significant contribution shift.

The mid-tier retailers (Southside Grocers, Prairie Provisions, Harbor Fresh) consistently outperform the nationals — their margins run 30–38%, driven by lower trade deduction intensity and minimal compliance fines.

Among distributors, UNFI and KeHE track closely at 17–25% contribution margins. Both show quarter-to-quarter noise driven by deduction timing, but no sustained trend. The distributor margin floor is structurally lower than retail — COGS ratios are slightly higher (60% vs. 55–59% for retailers), and spoilage fines add a cost layer retailers largely avoid.

DTC margins hold steady at 73% across all five quarters. The channel's cost structure is fundamentally different: COGS is 27% (versus 55–60% wholesale), and it faces zero deductions and zero fines. The margin advantage is permanent and structural — not a temporary condition.
