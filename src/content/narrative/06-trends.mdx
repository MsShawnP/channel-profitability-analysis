## Margin evolution

<p class="section-subtitle">Five quarters, Q1 2025 through Q1 2026</p>

Contribution margins across wholesale channels range from 44% to 55%. Retailers land between 46% and 55% quarter to quarter; distributors run lower at 44–46%. Quarter-to-quarter variation is modest — rarely more than two to three points — driven by fluctuations in deduction intensity and overhead timing.

Among retailers, Whole Foods has the strongest margin (55.1%), while Costco has the weakest (46.3%). The gap between the best and worst retailer in any given quarter is roughly nine points — wider than it appears, driven primarily by differences in COGS intensity and trade deduction rates.

The mid-tier retailers (Kroger, Sprouts, Regional Group) cluster between 50% and 53%. No persistent structural gap separates them — the spread is driven by short-term deduction timing rather than permanent cost differences.

Among distributors, UNFI, KeHE, and DPI Northwest cluster tightly at 44–46% contribution margins — roughly six to ten points below the retailer average. The conventional assumption that distribution is the cheaper route does not survive the full cost stack. DPI Northwest is the weakest quarter to quarter at 44.2%.

DTC margins hold steady near 83% year-round. The channel faces zero post-invoice deductions, zero fines, and zero dispute overhead — but its COGS ratio runs at 17.4%, comparable to retailers, because DTC sells at full retail pricing on the same product cost base. The result: DTC margins sit above both retailers and distributors, not between them.

<p class="chart-footnote">Source: Cinderhaven orders and deductions data, aggregated quarterly, FY2024–FY2026. Contribution margin = (revenue &minus; COGS &minus; deductions) / revenue. COGS ratio applied from annual channel averages. Compliance fines and operational overhead are excluded from the quarterly view due to reporting lags. Click any line to isolate a channel.</p>
