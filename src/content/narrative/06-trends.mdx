## Margin evolution

<p class="section-subtitle">Five quarters, Q1 2025 through Q1 2026</p>

Contribution margins across wholesale channels cluster in the 72–90% range. Retailers land between 80% and 85% quarter to quarter; distributors run higher at 89–92%. Quarter-to-quarter variation is modest — rarely more than two to three points — driven by fluctuations in deduction intensity and overhead timing.

Among retailers, all channels are remarkably stable. Sprouts shows the widest swing (81–84%), while Regional Group is the most consistent (82–83%). Costco has the lowest retailer margin (80–81%), but the gap between the best and worst retailer in any given quarter is only four to five points.

The mid-tier retailers (Kroger, Sprouts, Regional Group) and the nationals (Walmart, Costco) trade places depending on the quarter. No persistent structural gap separates them — the spread is driven by short-term deduction timing rather than permanent cost differences.

Among distributors, UNFI, KeHE, and DPI Northwest cluster tightly at 89–92% contribution margins — ten points above the retailer average. COGS ratios are lower (8% vs. 14–17% for retailers), and deduction intensity is minimal. DPI Northwest is the strongest quarter to quarter, consistently above 90%.

DTC margins hold steady at 92% year-round. The channel's cost structure is the simplest: COGS is 8% (comparable to distributors), and it faces zero deductions, zero fines, and zero dispute overhead. The margin advantage is permanent and structural — not a temporary condition.
