## Margin evolution

<p class="section-subtitle">Five quarters, Q1 2025 through Q1 2026</p>

Contribution margins across wholesale channels cluster in the 20–35% range — reflecting the structural reality that COGS consumes more than half of every wholesale dollar before deductions even begin. Quarter-to-quarter variation is driven almost entirely by deduction intensity.

Among retailers, Costco shows the most volatility: margins swing from 23% to 39% depending on the quarter's deduction load. A single quarter of concentrated promo billbacks or slotting fees can move the margin by fifteen points on Costco's revenue base. Walmart is more stable (20–25%), but at its scale, even a two-point move represents significant contribution shift.

The mid-tier retailers (Southside Grocers, Prairie Provisions, Harbor Fresh) generally outperform the nationals — their margins average 28–40%, driven by lower trade deduction intensity and minimal compliance fines.

Among distributors, UNFI and KeHE track closely at 21–27% contribution margins. Both show quarter-to-quarter noise driven by deduction timing, but no sustained trend. The distributor margin floor is structurally lower than retail — COGS ratios are slightly higher (60% vs. 53–59% for retailers), and spoilage fines add a cost layer retailers largely avoid.

DTC margins hold steady at 73% year-round. The channel's cost structure is fundamentally different: COGS is 27% (versus 53–60% wholesale), and it faces zero deductions and zero fines. The margin advantage is permanent and structural — not a temporary condition.
