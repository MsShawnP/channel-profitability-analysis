## Margin evolution

<p class="section-subtitle">Five quarters, Q1 2025 through Q1 2026</p>

Contribution margins across wholesale channels cluster in the 80–91% range. Retailers land between 80% and 83% quarter to quarter; distributors run higher at 89–91%. Quarter-to-quarter variation is modest — rarely more than two to three points — driven by fluctuations in deduction intensity and overhead timing.

Among retailers, all channels are remarkably stable. Costco has the lowest retailer margin (79–80%), while Whole Foods is typically the strongest (82–83%). The gap between the best and worst retailer in any given quarter is only three to four points.

The mid-tier retailers (Kroger, Sprouts, Regional Group) and the nationals (Walmart, Costco) trade places depending on the quarter. No persistent structural gap separates them — the spread is driven by short-term deduction timing rather than permanent cost differences.

Among distributors, UNFI, KeHE, and DPI Northwest cluster tightly at 89–91% contribution margins — roughly ten points above the retailer average. COGS ratios are lower (8–9% vs. 14–17% for retailers), and deduction intensity is minimal. DPI Northwest is the strongest quarter to quarter, consistently above 90%.

DTC margins hold steady near 83% year-round. The channel faces zero post-invoice deductions, zero fines, and zero dispute overhead — but its COGS ratio runs at 17.4%, comparable to retailers, because DTC sells at full retail pricing on the same product cost base. The result: DTC margins sit between retailers and distributors, not above both.

<p class="chart-footnote">Source: Cinderhaven orders and deductions data, aggregated quarterly, FY2024–FY2026. Contribution margin = (revenue &minus; COGS &minus; deductions) / revenue. COGS ratio applied from annual channel averages. Compliance fines and operational overhead are excluded from the quarterly view due to reporting lags. Click any line to isolate a channel.</p>
